Minimal Org Switcher — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 August 2026

In short: Minimal Org Switcher runs entirely inside your browser. It has no backend server, and the developer does not collect, receive, store, or sell any of your data. Everything the extension accesses stays on your device, and only leaves your browser when you actively use a feature that requires it (see below).

What the extension is

Minimal Org Switcher is a keyboard-launched command bar that overlays the page you're on, letting you quickly switch between the organisations you manage, jump to their internal pages, search your own history and bookmarks, and — optionally — control Spotify.

Data the extension accesses, and why

  • Browsing history & bookmarks — searched locally so you can jump to a page from the command bar. This is read on your device and is never transmitted anywhere.
  • Open tabs & the active tab — used to open the organisation or page you select in your browser.
  • Cookies — your existing session cookies are passed to sites you open inside the extension's in-overlay viewer, so those sites appear logged in exactly as they would in a normal tab. Cookies are read and replayed locally within your own browser; they are never sent to the developer or any third party.
  • Access to all sites — the command bar can be summoned on any page, and the in-overlay viewer can display any site you choose to open in it.
  • Spotify account (optional) — only if you choose to connect Spotify. The extension signs you in through Spotify's own OAuth screen and controls playback on your behalf.

When data leaves your browser

Data leaves your device in only two cases, both triggered by you using a specific feature:

  • Search suggestions — when the Google suggestion feature is active, the text you type is sent to Google's public autocomplete service to fetch suggestions. These requests are made without your cookies.
  • Spotify — if you connect Spotify, your search terms and playback commands are sent to the Spotify Web API, authenticated with a token stored locally on your device.

In both cases the data is handled by that third party (Google or Spotify) under their own privacy policies. It is not sent to, or seen by, the developer.

Data storage

Your settings, your organisation-usage ranking (used only to order results), cached lookup data, and any Spotify access token are stored locally in your browser via chrome.storage.local. They remain on your device and are removed when you uninstall the extension.

What we do not do

  • We do not operate a server or receive any of your data.
  • We do not sell or transfer your data to third parties.
  • We do not use your data for advertising, profiling, creditworthiness assessment, or any purpose unrelated to the command bar's single purpose.
