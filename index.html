The short version

  • The extension runs entirely in your browser. There is no backend server.
  • We don't collect, receive, store, or sell any of your data. There is nowhere for it to go.
  • Your history, bookmarks, tabs and cookies are read locally, only to make the bar work.
  • Two features talk to the outside world when you use them: Google search suggestions and Spotify. Those requests go straight from your browser to Google or Spotify, never through us.
  • Uninstall the extension and everything it stored is gone.

What the extension does

Minimal Org Switcher is a keyboard-launched command bar. It overlays the page you're on so you can switch between the organisations you manage, jump to their internal pages, search your own history and bookmarks, and control Spotify if you want to. All of it runs as code inside your browser. Nothing else is involved.

What it can reach, and why

Chrome will tell you the extension can access these things. Here is exactly what each one is for.

Browsing history and bookmarks

So you can search them from the bar. They are read locally on your device and never transmitted.

Open tabs and the active tab

To open the organisation or page you pick, in the right place.

Cookies

When you open a site inside the extension's viewer, your browser passes along your existing session cookies, so the site appears logged in exactly like it would in a normal tab. This happens locally, inside your own browser. Your cookies are never sent to us or to any third party.

Access to all sites

The bar can appear on any page, and the viewer can show any site you open in it. Chrome has no narrower permission for that.

Spotifyoptional

Only if you connect it. You sign in on Spotify's own page, through their official OAuth flow. We never see your password.

When anything leaves your browser

Exactly two features send data anywhere, and only while you use them.

Search suggestions

The text you type in the bar is sent to Google's public autocomplete service to fetch suggestions. The request is made without your cookies, and it falls under Google's privacy policy.

Spotify

If you connected Spotify, your search terms and playback commands go to the Spotify API, authenticated with a token stored on your device. That traffic falls under Spotify's privacy policy.

In both cases the request goes straight from your browser to Google or Spotify. Nothing passes through us, because there is nothing of ours in the middle.

What stays on your device

The extension keeps a small amount of data in Chrome's local storage:

  • your settings
  • a ranking of which organisations you use most, only to put the best match on top
  • cached data, so the bar opens fast
  • your Spotify token, if you connected Spotify

All of it lives in chrome.storage.local on your machine. Uninstall the extension and Chrome deletes it.

What we never do

  • We don't collect or receive your data. We couldn't. There is no server on our side.
  • We don't sell it, share it, or transfer it to anyone.
  • No advertising, no profiling, no tracking.
  • No use of your data to assess creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
